# Design Principles

These principles help us choose between options. Each one comes with a **test question** to ask of any feature, design or decision.

### 1. Art first, never an appliance
The hive must earn its place on the wall as a beautiful object even if the plants are not yet mature.
*Test: Would someone hang this in their living room if it held no plants?*

### 2. Plants are the hero
Everything else (structure, machinery, light, interface) exists to show off the plants and keep them healthy.
*Test: Does this draw the eye to the plants or away from them?*

### 3. Adding a cell is as easy as placing it
Expansion must need no tools, no plumbing knowledge and no configuration. The hive notices and adopts the new cell.
*Test: Could someone do this for the first time without instructions?*

### 4. Calm technology
The hive works quietly. It speaks up when something matters, and only then. Status should be readable at a glance, and the hive itself is the first place to look.
*Test: Does this add noise, light or notifications the owner didn't ask for?*

### 5. Depth on demand
The same hive serves the casual owner, the hobbyist and the obsessed. Defaults are excellent, and controls reveal themselves only to those who go looking.
*Test: Does the casual owner ever have to see this? Can the obsessed owner reach it?*

### 6. Forgiving by design
Water near walls and electricity is frightening. The hive must earn trust by failing gracefully: no floods, no stains, no dead plants after a missed week.
*Test: What happens if the owner ignores this for a month?*

### 7. Grows with you
The hive should be better, bigger and more personal over time, never obsolete. Cells bought years apart should work together.
*Test: Does this still make sense when the hive is five times bigger and three years older?*

### 8. Open to the curious *(proposed)*
Owners who want to integrate, automate and hack should be welcome to, without that being required.
*Test: Are we locking out a tinkerer for no good reason?*
