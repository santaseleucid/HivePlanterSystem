# Aesthetic Vision

How the hive should look and feel on the wall. This is direction, not specification. Visual references go in `05 Design/Moodboard/`.

## The feeling
- **Gallery, not garden centre.** It belongs next to art and furniture, not next to a hose.
- **Organic geometry.** Repeating cells give order, and the plants break it. The tension between grid and growth *is* the aesthetic.
- **Quiet materials.** Honest, tactile, warm. Nothing glossy, techy, or plasticky-looking.
- **Invisible machinery.** No visible tubes, cables, pumps or blinking LEDs.
- **Alive at night.** When lit, the hive could become a soft light sculpture rather than a dark patch on the wall.

## Composition
- A hive can grow in any direction and form organic, asymmetric shapes, not just rectangles.
- Cells can vary in depth, finish or planting density to create rhythm.
- Gaps and blank or art cells are part of the vocabulary.
- It should look intentional at every size, from 3 cells to 50.

## Finishes *(ideas to explore)*
- Natural: oak, walnut, cork, terracotta, concrete, stone
- Painted: muted palettes that let foliage dominate
- Statement: brass or bronze accents, mirror cells

## Geometry — open question
"Hive" suggests **hexagons**. Squares and other tessellations are also possible. The geometry is central to the product's identity and should be decided on aesthetic and experiential grounds first, then tested against practicality. See [[Open Questions]].
